Diane's 70th · Koningsdam · Wednesday, September 1, 2027

Glacier Bay National Park

Probably the best day of the trip, and it's included. For nine hours the Koningsdam is the tour: park rangers on board, more than 120 miles of the bay, and about an hour in front of a calving tidewater glacier. There's no port call, no going ashore and nothing to book.

The ship is the tour

No excursions and no going ashore. National Park Service rangers board early near Bartlett Cove, narrate over the PA and in the lounges, help spot wildlife and run a mobile visitor center (bring your park passport for the stamp). A Huna Tlingit cultural interpreter shares the stories of their ancestral homeland. Everything is included in the fare. The times below are typical, not a timetable.

  1. 6:15 amEnter the park. The rangers and the Tlingit interpreter come aboard near Bartlett Cove, just after sunrise.
  2. ~7–9:30Up the bay. South Marble Island for sea lions and puffins, then Gloomy Knob, where mountain goats graze on the cliffs. A good stretch for a balcony breakfast.
  3. ~10–noonTarr Inlet: Margerie and Grand Pacific glaciers, usually the turnaround. Expect about an hour in front of the ice, and the ship turns so both sides get the view.
  4. MiddayDutch pea soup on deck, a Holland America Glacier Bay tradition.
  5. ~Noon–1Johns Hopkins Inlet and Lamplugh Glacier, with Reid Glacier along the way.
  6. AfternoonBack down the bay. Ranger talks, a last look for whales, and time to get the passport book stamped.
  7. 3:15 pmThe rangers leave near Bartlett Cove and we turn south for Ketchikan.

On the Koningsdam

Where to watch from

There is no bad side. The ship turns at the glacier so port and starboard both get their turn. The choice is really between cold with the best view and warm behind glass.

The open bowColdest · closest

Holland America opens the bow for scenic cruising when the weather allows. Nothing between us and the ice. It fills early, and it's the windiest place on the ship.

The Crow's NestDeck 12, forward · warm

The panoramic lounge over the bow, and the best indoor spot. It has a café and a bar, and it's popular all cruise, so window seats go early on this day of all days.

The promenadeDeck 3 · sheltered

Close to the water, with doors back inside a few steps away. Be warned that on this ship the lifeboats cut into the view in places and there aren't many deck chairs.

Our own verandahPrivate · included

Because the ship turns, a balcony works well: our own chairs, our own bathroom and breakfast delivered. We see one side at a time, so pop up on deck if the rangers call something on the other.

Other good cornersDecks 9 and 11

The window tables in the Lido Market, and the open track on Deck 11 for a higher, all-round view.

Worth knowing

A possible bonus, a comparison and the evening

Three things that make this particular day on this particular route a little different.

A possible bonus

Johns Hopkins Inlet may reopen that morning

The inlet is closed to cruise ships while harbor seals are pupping and moulting on the ice. As we understand the park's rules, the closure runs through August 31, which would make September 1 the first day ships may go in.

We haven't confirmed the date with the park, and even if it's right the captain decides on the day, depending on ice and time. Treat it as a maybe. Otherwise we see Johns Hopkins Glacier from the mouth of the inlet.

Versus the Tracy Arm boat trip

This is the included, full glacier day

Two days earlier the ship pauses at the mouth of Tracy Arm so guests who bought the small-boat excursion (from $364, 7½–8½ hours) can transfer. That boat goes the length of a narrow fjord and gets closer to the ice than a big ship can. In 2026 it ran up Endicott Arm to Dawes Glacier instead, and 2027 will probably be the same.

Glacier Bay costs nothing extra and gives us nine hours, several tidewater glaciers, park rangers and our own cabins and coffee. That's why our advice is to skip the boat and keep the Juneau afternoon. Glacier lovers who want both won't regret it; it just isn't needed.

After 3:15 pm

An evening at sea

There is no evening port on this route. The ship heads about 280 nautical miles south to reach Ketchikan at 11:00 the next morning, so the rest of the day is ours: a nap, a swim under the Lido pool's glass roof, and sunset at about 8:10 pm.

A good night for a specialty dinner, since nobody is rushing back from a tour. Tamarind, Sel de Mer or Canaletto would all suit; the dining page has the details. Ketchikan doesn't start until 11, so there's no early alarm either.

Wildlife to look for

Bring binoculars · the rangers call out sightings

Sea lions and puffins

South Marble Island is the first wildlife stop of the morning, known for its sea lions and puffins.

South Marble IslandEarly morning

Mountain goats

Look for them on the cliffs of Gloomy Knob. Much easier with binoculars.

Gloomy KnobMid-morning

Humpback whales

Possible anywhere along the way. Watch for the blow first, then the back and the tail.

Anywhere in the bay

Sea otters

Small and low in the water, so they are easy to miss from a ship this size. Ask a ranger where to look.

On the water

Brown bears

Sometimes seen on the shoreline. They will be a long way off, so this is one for the binoculars.

Along the shore

Harbor seals

They pup and moult on the ice in Johns Hopkins Inlet, which is why the inlet is closed to cruise ships for part of the summer.

On the ice

Good to know

What to wear and bring

Dress warmly. On deck in Glacier Bay it's often 45–50°F with wind. Bring a hat, gloves and a windproof layer over something warm.

Bring. Binoculars, a camera, and a National Parks passport book if we have one.

Go early. The bow and the Crow's Nest fill early. If we want either, one of us should be there soon after the ship enters the park at 6:15.

The route isn't fixed. Which glaciers we see depends on ice and wildlife on the day; Margerie and Grand Pacific are the usual turnaround. The times on this page are typical ones.

Two ships a day. The park lets in only two cruise ships a day. Ours shares the bay with the Royal Princess, which is in from 6:00 am to 2:30 pm.

Nothing to buy. There are no excursions on this day and nowhere to go ashore. Holland America includes everything, so the only spending is on drinks or a specialty dinner that evening.
