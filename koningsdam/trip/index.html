Getting there & back

Flights, transfers and the hotel

This is the harder half of choosing the Koningsdam. Boston to Vancouver has no good nonstop, so both travel days are long. In return, the hotel sits on top of the cruise terminal and we never need a car on boarding day.

Flying out

Boston Logan → Vancouver (YVR) on Friday the 27th, with one stop. Take a 6–7 am departure on Air Canada via Toronto or Montreal, about 8 hours 20 to 8 hours 40 minutes in all, landing about noon–1 pm Pacific.

  • Why not the nonstop? JetBlue flies the only one, in summer only. On the 2026 schedule it left Boston about 5:10 pm and landed about 8:00 pm. That is too late for the birthday-eve dinner, and a delay would eat into the only buffer we have.
  • Other one-stops: a nonstop to Seattle on Alaska Airlines, Delta or JetBlue plus a one-hour hop gets in about 1:30–2:30 pm. Delta, United and American also connect through their hubs in about 8–9½ hours.
  • Via Toronto or Montreal we clear Canadian customs at that first airport, so leave a comfortable connection.
  • Rough fare: ~$450–700 per person round trip in economy. We've budgeted ~$600. Late-summer fares climb, so book in spring.
  • Fly the day before. Never fly in on the morning of a cruise.

The Canada Line runs from the airport to Waterfront Station every 7–12 minutes, with level boarding, elevators and luggage space. For four it costs about the same as one taxi. The taxi fare is a flat zone rate before tip, but four adults with big bags may need a van or two cars, and Holland America warns the airport taxi line can be long on cruise days.

The ship sails from Canada Place (999 Canada Place), right downtown. Everything we need on Friday and Saturday is within a ten-minute flat walk of it.

  • Money: Canadian dollars. Tap-to-pay cards work almost everywhere, including the train gates. Use a card with no foreign-transaction fee and choose to pay in CAD. C$1 is roughly US$0.72.
  • Tax: about 19% on hotel rooms, 5% on restaurant meals and 12% on most goods.
  • Tipping: 15–20% at restaurants, 10–15% for taxis, a few dollars a bag for porters and bell staff.

Flying home

  • Koningsdam arrives at 7:00 am on Sat Sep 4. Expect to be off between about 7:30 and 9:30 am by assigned group, then collect bags and clear Canadian customs in the terminal.
  • No Port Valet here. That service is Seattle only, so we take our own bags to the airport: Canada Line from Waterfront Station (a five-minute walk) or about 30 minutes by taxi. We should be at the airport between about 9:00 and 10:30 am.
  • US preclearance: we go through US customs at the Vancouver airport before boarding and land as a domestic flight. Allow three hours on a cruise Saturday. If we connect in Toronto or Montreal, preclearance happens there instead.
  • Earliest sensible flight: about 12:30 pm. A one-stop leaving 12:30–2:30 pm lands in Boston about 10:30 pm–midnight Eastern.
  • The alternative: JetBlue's nonstop is a red-eye, leaving 9:40 pm and landing about 6:00 am Sunday on the 2026 schedule. It buys a free day in Vancouver, but an overnight flight is a hard way for Mom to end the trip.

Our pick · on top of the terminal

Pan Pacific Vancouver

Built under the white sails of Canada Place, so the ship is downstairs. Harbour-view rooms look across to the North Shore mountains, Five Sails is in the building for dinner, and on Saturday Mom can watch the Koningsdam dock from the window. Bell staff take tagged bags from the room to the ship (reported at about C$5 a bag; confirm with the hotel).

~C$650–950 (~US$470–685) / night · 1–2 min indoors to the ship

Runner-up · across the street

Fairmont Waterfront

Harbour-view rooms, a heated rooftop pool and free delivery of tagged bags to the ship if we call before 11:30 am on sailing day. It is being renovated in stages, so ask what will be finished by August 2027. Often a little cheaper than the Pan Pacific.

~C$600–900 (~US$430–650) / night · 2–3 min covered walk

Easy logistics for less

Pinnacle Hotel Harbourfront

Many rooms have harbour and mountain views, and it sits beside the Coal Harbour seawall for an evening stroll. A flat roll to the terminal with bags, at a lower price than the two waterfront names.

~C$400–600 (~US$290–430) / night · 5–7 min flat walk

Rates are our estimates for one room on Friday, Aug 27, before tax. The Friday before a Saturday sailing is the busiest hotel night of the week in cruise season, so book a refundable rate early. The ship guide has the full hotel and restaurant lists.

Optional extras: Holland America's Have It All package bundles drinks, one specialty dinner, Wi-Fi and up to $100 per person of excursion credit for $65 per person per day ($1,820 for the four of us). Travel insurance is not optional on this trip: see the to-do list.

What to pack

Dressing for late-August Alaska

~73°Vancouver highs
~60°Juneau & Skagway
~50°Glacier Bay on deck
Rainlikely some days

Wear in layers

  • Waterproof rain jacket with a hood
  • Fleece or puffy mid-layer: the White Pass summit runs 10–15° colder than Skagway and is often windy
  • Warm hat & light gloves for Glacier Bay
  • Waterproof walking shoes
  • A light layer for Vancouver evenings by the water

On board

  • Two "Dressy night" outfits: collared shirt and slacks, a dress or skirt; jackets optional
  • Swimsuit for the spa's hydro pool
  • Binoculars, ideally one pair each
  • A small daypack for port days

Documents

  • Passports (all four), in hand luggage, never in a checked bag
  • Cruise documents & luggage tags
  • Excursion confirmations
  • Travel insurance details
  • A credit card with no foreign-transaction fee

Nice to have

  • Camera with a zoom lens
  • Motion-sickness remedies for the open-water sea day
  • Reusable water bottle
  • A phone plan that includes Canada; airplane mode at sea
  • A birthday banner for the cabin door!

No plug adapter is needed: Canada uses the same outlets as home. Cannabis is legal in British Columbia but illegal to carry across the border in either direction.
